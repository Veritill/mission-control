What each step is

  1. Sign-up: two questions before Getting started opens. The kind of business (one chip) adopts that kind's departments, shelves and product families at once; then food made here: a store is asked "do you also make and sell food here, hot or cold?", a restaurant is asked the cuisine (or lets the menu read answer it). A restaurant without a cuisine still gets the restaurant base; the cuisine adds to it later.
  2. Give: one key or one drop, then "Right?" on what was read. Business type, cuisine or store kind, vendors and hours are never asked.
  3. Check: the judgement taps, ten at a time, inside the card. A grocery: items, SNAP flags, shelf prices. A restaurant: dishes, the prep list, recipes, the chef's pass. A grocery never sees a dish; a restaurant never sees a price tag.
  4. Costs: use the POS costs? how many months? how do each vendor's invoices arrive? then the sitting (item-led for a store, dish-led for a kitchen), then the result: the menu costed dish by dish for a kitchen (cost per serving, price, margin, red rows first), the shelves costed for a store. Costing is finished inside onboarding, never left for later; the cost-gap loop only chases the few red rows.
  5. Open: the team, the till (a restaurant on Toast keeps Toast as its till), the online store if wanted, Go live.
  6. Back and forth: a folded done row reopens on tap, with its answer editable; the current card stays below it. Nothing is ever lost by going back.
  7. After go live: the page becomes "What runs by itself" plus the agent's card for the few asks that outlive setup. A store's first count lives here, never as a gate to opening.
