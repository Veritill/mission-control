Mockup #239 — Grocery storefront: browse · cart · checkout

Shopkiraana's public, no-sign-in customer door (docs/requirements/commerce.md §8) — one screen, three steps: browse adds to a cart drawer, the drawer picks pickup/delivery, checkout confirms and places the order. Live demo: tap ✚ Add on a couple of items, tap the black cart strip, choose Delivery and an address, then Checkout.

Against the eight screens.md §0 checks

1. One jobThe whole flow is one job — "order something" — done in three sequential steps (browse → cart → checkout), the same way the invoice workspace is one screen with tabs, not three unrelated screens. Each step's own action is visible with no scrolling: ✚ Add, Checkout, Place order.
2. No paragraphsEvery row is a name, a price and a chip. The only sentence-length text is the tax note ("computed per item — SNAP-eligible items are tax-exempt") and the payment note ("pay when you get your order"), both under twelve words and both sitting where an ⓘ would — not blocking the job.
3. Icon on every rowEvery catalog row (🌾🫘🧀🧈🌶️🥬🥟), every cart line, and every action carries an icon (🛍️ pickup, 🚚 delivery, 💳 pay, ✅ placed, ✚/−/+ steppers).
4. ≤7 thingsBrowse: store header, search, category-chip row, ~4 visible item rows, cart strip — one job's worth of controls, same bundling the register mockup (#135) used. Cart drawer: title/close, pickup/delivery toggle, address block (delivery only), the line list, Checkout button ≈ 6. Checkout: back + recap, contact, time, order summary, payment note, Place order = 7 exactly.
5. Fast, no on-open summaryTotals here are pure arithmetic over cart state already held in memory (computeTotals()) — no recompute trip. The real build reads price via pricing.ts's resolvePrice (storefrontOf) and tax per line's own character the way checks.ts's resolveCheck already does for the register — never one flat cart-wide rate. No screen exists yet to measure against a 300 ms Playwright budget (CLAUDE.md: never guess a number) — that spec is a build-time follow-up, timed on the page's own clock the way apps/web/e2e/kitchen.spec.ts already had to learn to do, not across a Playwright click().
6. A way backBrowse is the flow's own home — it has no parent, the same as the sign-in gate. The cart drawer's ✕ returns to Browse; Checkout's ← returns to the cart with fulfilment and contact details intact; Confirmation's "Back to store" returns to Browse. No dead ends.
7. One home per functionSearch/browse lives only on Browse. Quantity editing lives only in the cart drawer's steppers (Browse's own rows just add/increment the same cart, never a second edit surface). Fulfilment (pickup/delivery, address) is SET only in the cart drawer; Checkout shows it read-only with a tap that routes back rather than a duplicate control — the exact defect this check warns against.
8. As few screens as possibleThe cart is a drawer over Browse, not a page — check 8's own preferred fix. Checkout is the one distinct job (confirm identity + fulfilment + pay before an order is created) that earns its own full step, kept to ONE screen, not a wizard. Total: one new screen (the storefront) with a drawer and one follow-on step — matches commerce.md §10 exactly ("one job … at most seven things per screen"). The staff-side orders hub is explicitly out of scope here (folds into the existing /catering book per §10, a separate later issue).
  • Availability honesty (commerce.md §8, O1): every sellable row reads "Usually in stock," never a fabricated quantity, until a tenant's opening-count switch is on; Paneer demonstrates the other true state, "Out of stock today," with its Add control removed rather than left clickable.
  • Mixed tax (commerce.md §6): Basmati Rice and Toor Dal are tax-exempt/SNAP-eligible lines (🟢 SNAP chip, $0 tax); Ghee, Turmeric, Garam Masala, Okra and Samosas are taxable — the checkout total sums tax per line's own character, never one flat percentage, so the two SNAP items in the demo cart provably do not inflate the tax line.
  • Delivery fee vs. unmapped (commerce.md §4): the address presets show both real paths — an in-range address prices a mile-tier fee ($2.99/$4.99/$7.99, the same deliveryFeeFor shape catering already uses), and an out-of-range address (Nashville) refuses plainly ("delivery isn't available at this address yet") instead of guessing a fee — Checkout stays disabled until the cart resolves one way or the other.
  • Payment (commerce.md §6, D5 undecided): this mockup assumes option 3 — pay at pickup/delivery, no card entered online — since it needs no new Stripe connector and is the only one of the three shapes that is free to build; the checkout screen names this plainly rather than implying a decision Kiran has not made. A capture-at-placement or authorize-then-capture flow (options 1/2) would replace this one row with a card field once D5 is answered — it does not change the screen count.
